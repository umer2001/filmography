# Screenplay Review Report

## Review Metadata

- Project: Borrowed Light
- Screenplay file: `docs/examples/example-screenplay.md`
- Screenplay version reviewed: v1
- Reviewer: Filmography Example
- Review date: 2026-06-06
- Review type: Blocking gate
- Ready for human approval: With revisions

## 1. Skeleton And Structural Integrity

- Point A to Point B transformation: Clear but understated. Sana begins emotionally shut down and ends willing to let Ray remain present, but the intermediate pressure could be sharper.
- Identity: Sana's routine and guardedness are established efficiently in the diner and bus stop scenes.
- Conflict: The broken trust around the cassette is visible, but Ray's opposing pressure is mostly implied until the final scene.
- Choice: Sana's "Then stay" lands as a choice, though Ray needs one earlier visible action that costs him something.
- Change: The ending shifts from avoidance to cautious repair without over-resolving the relationship.
- Structural verdict: Strong emotional spine, but the draft should clarify Ray's active choice before human approval.

## 2. Attention Mechanics And Retention Loops

- Opening hook: The empty cassette player is a good visual question, but the first image could imply a sharper emotional consequence.
- Emotional root targeted: Regret, abandonment, and the hope that repair is still possible.
- Information gaps and open loops: The missing cassette creates a clean open loop. Ray seeing the cassette in SC-03 reopens that loop before the final handoff.
- Flat zones or likely drop-off points: SC-02 risks becoming purely atmospheric unless the bus passing without Sana boarding reveals a specific avoidance pattern.
- Why / How / What clarity: Why matters is clear; how repair happens is mostly through object return and presence; what payoff is a fragile decision to stay.
- Retention verdict: Functional and restrained, with room for a stronger cold-open curiosity gap.

## 3. Visual Skin And Perspective Audit

- Strong visual storytelling moments: Cassette player without tape, Sana skipping the bus, Ray stopping at his apartment door, the dented cassette on the laundromat counter.
- Exposition-heavy moments: Ray's "I should've come back sooner" states the wound plainly.
- Visual metaphor opportunities: Let Ray hesitate over the cassette before speaking, or show the dent catching fluorescent light before the apology.
- Pattern breaks: Ray's rainy entrance breaks Sana's routine effectively.
- Visual verdict: The draft is visually grounded, but one line of exposition should become behavior or object handling.

## 4. Emotional Pacing And Bonding

- Emotional curve: Quiet isolation, delayed guilt, confrontation, restrained invitation.
- Relatability: The everyday spaces and small object stakes are easy to enter.
- Early reason to keep watching: The empty cassette player suggests a missing relationship and invites curiosity.
- Pattern twists: The ending avoids full reconciliation, which preserves emotional honesty.
- Empathy or bonding strength: Sana bonds through silence and fatigue; Ray needs one more specific sign of accountability.
- Emotional pacing verdict: Emotionally coherent, but the middle needs a stronger pressure beat.

## 5. Actionable Directives For The Screenwriter

### Stop Doing

- Do not rely on apology dialogue as the main proof that Ray has changed.

### Start Doing

- Give Ray one visible, costly action before the laundromat that shows he has chosen repair over self-protection.

### Refine Specific Scenes By

- SC-01: Make the empty cassette player feel more emotionally loaded in the first image.
- SC-02: Tie Sana missing the bus to a concrete avoidance choice, not just loneliness.
- SC-03: Let Ray's hesitation expose guilt before he reaches the laundromat.
- SC-04: Convert part of Ray's apology into a tactile action with the cassette.

## 6. Blocking Issues

- Critical story issues: None.
- Important revision needs: Strengthen Ray's active choice and sharpen the cold-open hook.
- Minor polish notes: Preserve the restraint; avoid making the final beat too romantic or resolved.

## 7. Final Assessment

- Ready for human approval: With revisions
- Reasoning: The screenplay has a clear emotional spine and strong visual motif, but the review gate should hold until Ray's accountability is more visible and the opening hook carries more force.
- Required next step: Screenwriter revision, then re-run `screenplay-reviewer`.
