# Story Foundation

## 1. Project Identity
- Project: Borrowed Light
- Version: v1
- Date: 2026-06-06

## 2. Core Concept
- Premise: Two estranged collaborators reconnect through a damaged cassette that forces them to confront trust, failure, and repair.
- What is the story about at its heart? A story about earning the right to return.
- Why this story should exist: It uses small urban spaces and everyday objects to tell an emotionally restrained reconciliation story.

## 3. Logline
- Logline: When a withdrawn diner worker is visited by the friend who once vanished with her demo tape, the late-night return of the damaged cassette forces both of them to choose whether repair is still possible.

## 4. Synopsis
- Short synopsis: Sana and Ray, once bound by a shared musical dream, have drifted into silence after Ray disappeared while carrying Sana's only demo cassette. Weeks later he returns with the tape, and the story follows the slow, unspectacular process of deciding whether trust can be rebuilt.
- Longer synopsis: In a rainy contemporary city, Sana works long shifts and avoids the unfinished parts of her life. Ray, ashamed of a failure he cannot easily explain, re-enters her orbit carrying the dented cassette that symbolizes everything he dropped. Across a string of intimate spaces and low-key confrontations, they move from avoidance to honesty, and from honesty to a fragile willingness to stay in the same future.

## 5. Emotional Promise
- What the audience should feel by the end: Quiet relief mixed with uncertainty
- Primary tonal range: Intimate, grounded, restrained

## 6. Early Story Shape
- Beginning: Sana lives inside routine while Ray is absent but unresolved
- Middle: Ray reappears and is forced to prove his return through action
- End: The relationship opens again without pretending the damage is gone
- Key turning points: Ray's return; the cassette handoff; the choice to stay

## 7. Project-Level Questions To Lock Before Screenplay
- Character questions: How much guilt does Ray reveal directly?
- World questions: How often does rain recur as a visual motif?
- Visual questions: How cool and fluorescent should the visual world stay before warmth appears?
- Format questions: Is this a short film or a proof-of-concept sequence?
